Learning Marginal Viewpoint Utility for Budgeted Evidence Selection in RAG

Yiyang Duan, Ziqi Yu, Tiantong Wu, Yebo Wu, Wei Yang Bryan Lim
26 minread4.30score

Ask about electoral participation and a complete answer should cover three groups: people who voted, people who did not, and people who were ineligible. A context made only of voters' responses represents one viewpoint, even if every record is accurate and relevant. Under a 1,024-token context budget, classic MMR repeatedly picks evidence supporting the same three viewpoints for a WVS corruption-perception query and leaves out "no corruption perceived". This paper from Nanyang Technological University and the University of Toronto proposes LMVU, a linear ranker with just 18 coefficients that selects evidence by how many uncovered viewpoints it adds to the current context. On four viewpoint-annotated benchmarks, it reaches the exact oracle's full coverage of 1.0000 on the protected WVS fold and a four-benchmark mean RVC of 0.9588, above all 16 non-oracle methods, including two neural rerankers that cost orders of magnitude more to train.

1. The problem: relevant is not the same as covering

RAG usually ranks passages by query relevance and puts the top ones in the context. But some queries need coverage of different answers or positions, which the authors call viewpoints: an answer category or position the evidence supports. Highly relevant evidence may all support the same viewpoint and leave others unrepresented. MMR trades relevance against similarity to already-selected evidence, and recent high-diversity RAG selection follows the same idea, but semantic diversity is not viewpoint coverage: differently worded evidence can support one viewpoint, and similar wording can express opposing ones.

Formally, a query q has a full candidate pool $E(q)$ and an annotated viewpoint inventory Vq​; each item e has token cost $c(e)$ and supports a viewpoint subset $V(e)$. The goal is to maximize recovered viewpoint coverage RVC(S)=∣⋃e∈S​V(e)∣/∣Vq​∣ subject to ∑c(e)≤B, crediting each viewpoint once. The key constraint is label blindness: gold viewpoint annotations are for training and evaluation only and are invisible at inference, so the selector must estimate coverage gains from observable evidence features and the current selection state. With gold access, the authors solve the full-pool optimum Sq⋆​ exactly by viewpoint-subset dynamic programming, so they can measure how far any allocator is from optimal: its headroom.

2. Method: learning marginal viewpoint utility

Target signal. What an allocator really needs is a candidate's marginal gain given the current selection, ΔV(e∣q,S)=∣V(e)∖V(S)∣. The same candidate's gain changes as the selection grows and drops to zero once its viewpoints are covered. ΔV needs gold labels and cannot be deployed, but it is computable on development data and can supervise a surrogate scorer fθ​(q,S,e).

Training states. On frozen development pools (MMR-oversampled at λ=0.5), five policies are rolled out: dense-relevance greedy, live MMR, two pinned-seed random orders, and a gold-guided marginal-gain policy. Prefix states of up to nine steps are snapshotted and deduplicated, and each state scores at most 20 candidates (dense top-10 plus a deterministic uniform draw), with gold labels giving realized gains.

Scoring and learning. fθ​=θ⊤ϕ~​(q,S,e) is linear in 18 features in four groups: candidate descriptors (4), evidence–state geometry (5), atomic–state geometry (5) and shared state descriptors (4). All similarity geometry reuses the frozen embeddings already computed for dense scoring, adding no model dependency. The static ablation M0 uses only three features: dense relevance, query–candidate cosine and candidate length. Shared state descriptors are identical for all candidates in a state, cancel in the differences and get exactly zero coefficients, so the budget acts only through the feasibility filter, not the score.

Training avoids pointwise regression because gain scales differ across states (from zero to the number of uncovered viewpoints). The authors form ordered pairs (e+,e−) only within a state, with ΔV(e+)>ΔV(e−), at most 20 per state and ties dropped; each pair yields two antisymmetric examples fit with an L2-regularized pairwise logistic (Bradley–Terry) loss, convex, by LBFGS with C=1.0. Differencing cancels state-specific additive offsets, and the pairwise loss optimizes exactly the decision the allocator faces at each step. Unlike MMR's fixed λ, LMVU's weights are fixed at inference but act on geometry features that update with the selection, so the effective novelty penalty varies with what is covered: a candidate can rank high early and drop once its viewpoint enters the selection.

Allocation. Starting from an empty context, each step takes the argmax only among candidates whose full cost fits the remaining budget, adds it and updates the state, and stops when nothing fits; evidence is never truncated. Each step costs O(∣Ft​∣⋅18), maintained with incremental statistics and no pool-level similarity matrix. The authors state plainly that LMVU inherits only greedy's feasibility guarantee and has no coverage approximation bound.

3. Experimental setup

Four benchmarks: survey-derived WVS and ESS10, claim–perspective Perspectrum, and argument-key-point ArgKP (a saturation control). Mean full-pool sizes at budget 1024 are 507, 9, 76 and 88, and evaluation queries number 6, 97, 6 and 34. Budgets are 512, 1024 and 2048, with 1024 primary. WVS uses the protected respondent fold USA 4, which allows only predeclared evaluations and read-only audits, never fitting. The encoder is a frozen local all-MiniLM-L6-v2. Besides breadth RVC₁, the paper reports repeated support RVC₂: a viewpoint with one supporting source unit gets 0.5, and two or more distinct units get 1. Baselines are Random, BM25, FacLoc, xQuAD, SubMix, LexRank, Dense, CeRerank, Cluster, MMR and M0, plus AdaGReS, MMKP and the neural rerankers JPR and RichRAG under the same protocol. Audits confirm no runtime gold-field access across 28,314 per-query allocator runs. The authors also note that, given prior held-out inspection, this full-pool extension is exploratory rather than a fresh confirmatory test.

MMR's headroom to the exact oracle is 0.083, 0.069 and 0.157 on WVS, Perspectrum and ESS10, and zero on ArgKP. LMVU recovers all of it on WVS, gains 0.0466 over MMR on the headroom-richest ESS10 (30% recovered), and ties the ceiling on ArgKP. Three observations: lexical relevance alone (BM25) trails embedding methods on WVS and Perspectrum; structured diversity heuristics are benchmark-dependent, e.g. MMKP beats MMR on Perspectrum but collapses to 0.2502 on ESS10 as its per-cluster cap binds; and only the learned state-dependent utility beats MMR on all three headroom-positive benchmarks.

On significance, paired differences against MMR are nonnegative on every benchmark, but only the Perspectrum repeated-support gain (+0.0213, 95% CI [+0.007, +0.037]) has an interval excluding zero. Against other baselines, the larger folds give significance, e.g. on ESS10 +0.170 over JPR and +0.225 over AdaGReS (p<0.001). The counterexample is RichRAG overtaking LMVU on ESS10 (−0.068, p=0.004). Both neural methods win only single benchmarks: JPR reaches the ArgKP ceiling already at 512 tokens, RichRAG leads ESS10 breadth at every budget, yet both trail MMR on WVS and Perspectrum. The cost gap is huge: on one L20 GPU, JPR takes 150.5 s per fit and RichRAG (SFT+DPO) 962.7 s, plus about 11 GPU-hours of neural inference for evaluation; LMVU refits its 18 coefficients on CPU in seconds (1.3 s measured for the largest benchmark's 13,952 development states) and scores each candidate with one 18-dimensional dot product.

5. State conditioning, transfer and boundaries

What state contributes. LMVU beats static M0 by 0.202 on WVS, 0.075 on ArgKP and 0.061 on ESS10; Perspectrum is the exception, where M0 is 0.004 higher. A capacity-matched null, M1-SHUF, keeps all 18 features but shuffles the 14 state features within each state, and development ranking collapses to the static level (WVS NDCG@5 0.6002 vs LMVU's 0.7944), so the gain comes from state information, not capacity. Removing the six atomic-granularity features drops WVS development NDCG@5 from 0.6597 to 0.4418.

Cross-benchmark transfer. Trained leave-one-benchmark-out on the other three benchmarks' development states, the model still beats MMR on development folds by 0.150 on WVS, 0.044 on Perspectrum and 0.037 on ESS10, with full coverage on WVS, suggesting the coefficients capture a reusable coverage regularity.

Repeated support. Under RVC₂ the ordering mostly holds, but on WVS the relevance-free FacLoc edges ahead (0.9167 vs 0.9015). Depth holds more residual headroom: on WVS LMVU's breadth is at the full-pool optimum while depth is still 0.0985 short; ESS10 has a 0.1107 breadth gap and 0.1544 depth gap.

Budgets and encoders. The WVS gain over MMR is largest at 512 tokens (+0.117), +0.083 at 1024 and narrows to +0.042 at 2048, as the relaxed budget lets MMR buy the missing viewpoints; at 1024 the whole WVS gain sits in the corruption-perception issue group (+0.250). Realized gains track available headroom (Pearson r=0.574, n=4, descriptive only) and vanish where the ceiling is met. Results shift with the encoder: with bge-base, +0.025 on Perspectrum and +0.074 on ESS10 with intervals excluding zero; with bge-m3, smaller gains with intervals crossing zero, and −0.0417 on WVS.

6. Limitations and industry relevance

The authors acknowledge that performance depends on the embedding pipeline and on viewpoint annotations for training and evaluation, that the full-pool extension is exploratory, that the learned scorer has no coverage approximation guarantee, and that downstream generation and adaptation to domains with new viewpoint inventories remain unevaluated. Note too how small the evaluation is: WVS and ArgKP have only 6 queries each, the WVS "full coverage" comes from a 6-query protected fold, most differences against MMR are not significant, and the conclusions are encoder-sensitive.

For engineering teams building RAG, the value is a cheap, auditable selection layer: no neural reranker, just 18 features from existing embeddings, seconds to train and a dot product at inference, a drop-in replacement for MMR between retrieval and context assembly. It suits survey analysis, public-opinion and policy Q&A, where every side needs to appear, provided a batch of viewpoint-annotated development data is available. The more transferable lesson is the evaluation method: compute headroom with an exact oracle to confirm whether and how much coverage can improve before switching selectors; where headroom is zero, as on ArgKP, no method gains anything.
