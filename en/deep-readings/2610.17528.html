ATTUNE: Agentic Throughput Tuning for LLM Training

Leo Fan, Hongyang Wang, Wenbo Chen, Jingguo Liu, Xian Li, Xuan Wang, Ruochen Wen, Yiming Yan, Hao Wu, Yichuan Li, Qian Qu, Zheshen Wang, Benoit Dumoulin
5 minread4.45score

LLM training throughput hinges on the recipe—sharding, memory–compute trade-offs, kernels and overlap—and that recipe changes with every model, accelerator and framework. Fixed-knob tuners only check that a job runs, miss levers outside the preset space, and can silently change training semantics. Amazon’s ATTUNE is a coding agent that retunes any working recipe unattended inside a GPU-hour budget. On H100/H200/B200 it lifts throughput 1.7× on average (up to ~3.9×) and beats four NVIDIA AutoModel published recipes by 6–17%; on held-out models it improves recipes written for another setting by up to 2.6× (MiniMax-M2). Throughput gaps often live outside the fixed knob space—letting a coding agent edit the recipe beats grid search.

1. Problem

Bayesian/grid tuners search preset knobs for “runs.” Missed throughput often sits in recipe text (expert capacity, overlap, kernels). ATTUNE searches by editing code/config.

2. Method

One agent runs a reason–act loop: hypothesize → one change → profile → diagnose. Deterministic gates outside the agent refuse objective-changing flags, validate measurements, and decide whether a candidate beats the leader; a final consistency check follows. Budget is capped in GPU-hours.

3. Core claim

Tuning becomes recipe editing with gates that block silent training changes. Editable recipe text vs immutable training semantics—gates separate the two.

4. Experiments

Mean +1.7× (peak ~3.9×) vs replaced recipes; +6–17% vs AutoModel published numbers; held-out MiniMax-M2 up to +2.6×; beats automated tuners including Bayesian opt under the same GPU-hour budget.

5. Limits

Needs a working seed recipe and parseable profiles; gate rules track framework versions; throughput ≠ unchanged loss curves.

6. Practice

Hang recipe agents on new-model×new-cluster onboarding behind a gate allowlist and a GPU-hour cap.
