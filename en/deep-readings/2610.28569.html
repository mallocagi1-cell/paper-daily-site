ECHO: Efficient Cascade with Homogeneous Operators for Distillation-Guided 100K-Sequence Modeling

Wenhao Li, Qinglin Jia, Chenghou Jin, Zihong Xian, Jiezhou Ji, Yanzhi Liu, Zezheng Wang, Ruiming Tang, Chaoyi Ma, Zhaojie Liu, Wenwu Ou
16 minread4.75score

ECHO makes very long behavior histories usable by selecting reusable original events before ranking, then retrieving evidence for each candidate from that smaller collection. Kuaishou separates user-level selection from online target-aware matching. Item identities, timestamps and side features survive selection, so a ranker can still trace a prediction to individual historical interactions.

1. Motivation: useful history becomes expensive when every candidate reads it

A recent-history window can miss old but persistent interests. Reading the full history for every candidate avoids that truncation, but computation grows with both the history length and the number of candidates. Compressing everything into a few latent vectors limits cost, while making individual evidence harder to retrieve.

ECHO reduces the searchable set rather than replacing every event with a mixed latent state. The difficulty is deciding what to retain before the online candidate is known. The selector must preserve a useful range of interests while leaving candidate-specific decisions to a later stage.

2. Method: select for the user, retrieve for the candidate

The Distilled Selector scores historical events using user features and writes the retained records to shared storage. Online requests read that state rather than executing selection or scanning the entire history again. Multiple candidates reuse the same retained collection.

The production configuration retains 10,000 events per user and passes 100 candidate-relevant events to final aggregation. These are distinct budgets: one determines the breadth of accessible evidence, while the other bounds detailed online matching.

A full-history teacher conditions on the training candidate and produces a relevance distribution over events. A user-only student learns from those target-specific distributions across training examples. It learns a reusable retention policy, not the final answer for a particular candidate.

Distribution matching can still leave important events just outside the selection boundary. An auxiliary objective pairs teacher-preferred missed events with retained events the teacher considers less useful. It pushes those missed events above the inclusion threshold. The teacher and student have separate event encoders; distillation transfers the relevance signal.

The online retriever then searches only the retained records. It shares the teacher's relevance parameters with the downstream target-attention scorer, reducing disagreement between retrieval and aggregation. Deployment optimizations share common user inputs, compact the stored records and move key-side projection to the query side using an equivalent inner product. This avoids materializing a projected key for every retained position.

3. Contribution: preserve access to evidence without copying the teacher's ordering

The selector does not need to reproduce the teacher's exact fine-grained ranking. It needs a sufficiently broad retained set that includes the evidence a later candidate-aware retriever will need.

The preservation results support this separation of responsibilities. They measure fidelity to the teacher, however, rather than complete coverage of real user interests. Evidence the teacher misses is not recovered automatically.

Unlike a recency cache, the retained collection is learned from prediction-related supervision. Unlike latent compression, surviving events remain individually addressable. That also creates an inspectable route from a recommendation back to concrete behavior.

4. Results: distinguish ranking quality, analytical compute and online impact

The paper evaluates a public benchmark and Kuaishou production data. GAUC measures discrimination within user groups. The table selects four tasks and uses the strongest baseline for each task.

For public comment prediction, (0.6347−0.6252)/0.6252 is approximately 1.52%. The absolute GAUC change is 0.0095; it is not a 1.52 percentage-point increase.

A seven-day online experiment allocated 5% of production traffic to treatment and reported a 0.613% increase in app usage time. The control already included advanced long-sequence retrieval and compression, making this an incremental improvement over an established system.

The roughly 160-fold reduction refers to analytical sequence-branch FLOPs relative to STCA. It is not a measured whole-service throughput or latency multiplier. In a separate length study, STCA still achieves higher accuracy at longer histories. ECHO trades some full-history modeling capacity for bounded online cost.

Removing both distillation and the boundary auxiliary loss produces the largest deterioration on comment prediction. That supports explicit retention supervision, although removing both objectives together does not isolate the auxiliary term's individual effect.

5. Limitations: discarded events are unavailable online

The authors explicitly identify long-tail loss: a relevant event removed by user-level selection cannot be recovered by the downstream retriever. Original-event access applies only to records that survived.

Selection adapts through training and refreshes, rather than to each online candidate. Recovering a neglected interest requires relevant training signals, model updates and a refreshed retained collection. Newly introduced candidate types and abrupt interest changes therefore deserve separate retention-quality checks. Proprietary industrial data also prevent reproduction of the complete production system.

6. Practical implications

Compare learned retention against recent-window and random selection under the same storage budget. Measure important-event recall, evidence age and coverage across user-interest groups, alongside CTR or GAUC. This reveals where the retained capacity goes.

For community search, long-term behavioral evidence could form a separate ranking branch while query semantics and fresh actions remain explicit inputs. Inspect whether rare queries lose a small amount of decisive historical evidence.

Operationally, define how quickly new interactions reach the retained state, how model versions align with stored records and how rollback handles old representations. Moving computation outside the request path makes state freshness part of ranking quality.
