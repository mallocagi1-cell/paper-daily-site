RACE: RoPE-Aligned Attention Compression Based on Query-Key Activation Energies

Irene Tenison, Abdulrahman Alabdulkareem, Daniel Karl I. Weidele, Nima Dehmamy, Dan Gutfreund
26 minread4.30score

KV cache size is tokens × layers × head dimension × precision. Cutting tokens (H2O, SnapKV) and cutting precision (KIVI) are well studied. Methods that cut the head dimension exist too, but most of them save only storage: EigenAttention and PALU store keys as low-dimensional vectors, then reconstruct full-dimensional keys at inference time so that RoPE positional encoding can be applied. The paper's Table 1 quantifies the cost: at 50% KV retention, EigenAttention's decode FLOPs are 11.42× those of standard attention and PALU's are 21.92×. RACE (RoPE-Aligned Attention Compression based on activation Energy) takes a different route: keep only complete RoPE rotary pairs, choose them by the joint activation energy of queries and keys, and fold the choice straight into the weights. RoPE, caching and attention then all run in the reduced dimension, with no reconstruction and no custom kernel. On Llama-3.2-3B at 50% KV retention and 256K context, total inference FLOPs drop 47.8%, overall throughput rises 1.79×, time to first token (TTFT) falls 43.2% and GPU memory falls 28.1%.

1. The problem: storage saved, compute not

Why is head-dimension compression hard? Attention scores depend on Q~​K~⊤ after RoPE. RoPE treats every two adjacent coordinates of a head as a "rotary pair" on a 2-D plane and rotates it by an angle that depends on token position. If a dense low-rank matrix projects keys to low dimension before RoPE, that projection generally does not commute with the rotation: projecting then rotating differs from rotating then projecting. Methods like EigenAttention and PALU must therefore rebuild full-dimensional keys before RoPE. PALU writes a custom kernel so the reconstructed tensor is never materialized, but decode FLOPs are still dominated by the reconstruction basis.

Two other approaches each miss a piece. Thin Keys avoids key reconstruction but leaves value dimensions untouched, so KV savings stop at 75% retention. The concurrent RAP also keeps complete rotary pairs, but scores them with empirical Fisher from gradients of WK​, which needs backpropagation and looks only at the key side.

The authors frame the task as constrained approximate matrix multiplication: find a low-dimensional map P with (Q~​P)(K~P)⊤≈Q~​K~⊤, subject to the map being executable before RoPE. Two design principles follow. Queries and keys should share one low-dimensional subspace, because their product is taken along the same feature dimension. And the subspace should be chosen from statistics of both queries and keys, because the quantity being approximated is their product.

2. Method: keep complete rotary pairs, rank them by joint energy

Which reductions commute with RoPE? Theorem 1 gives the exact answer for coordinate selection (keeping some coordinates and dropping the rest): it commutes with RoPE if and only if the kept coordinates form a union of complete rotary pairs, ordered consistently with the original RoPE. The intuition is direct. The two coordinates of a pair mix during rotation, so keeping only one of them loses the part mixed in from the other. When the condition holds, rotating the reduced vector by the original frequencies gives exactly the same result as rotating in full dimension and then selecting coordinates.

Which pairs to keep? For the m-th rotary pair of each attention head, compute the joint energy on calibration data:
Em​=∥Qm​∥F2​+∥Km​∥F2​
Qm​ and Km​ are the query and key activations of all calibration tokens on that pair of coordinates, and ∥⋅∥F​ is the Frobenius norm. For target dimension rk​, keep the rk​/2 pairs with the highest energy. Proposition 1 shows that the product error from dropping a set of pairs is bounded by 21​∑Em​ over the dropped pairs, so taking the top-energy pairs minimizes this separable bound exactly. The authors are explicit that this is not the same as minimizing the true error; it is a cheap, forward-only criterion.

Values. Values are not rotated, so RACE follows EigenAttention: run SVD on calibration activations and use the top rv​ right singular vectors as PV​.

Folding into weights. Once chosen, WQ​ and WK​ are right-multiplied by PQK​, WV​ by PV​, and WO​ is left-multiplied by PV⊤​. The result is an ordinary attention layer with a smaller head dimension. It caches low-dimensional K and V, applies RoPE only to the kept pairs (keeping their original frequency indices rather than renumbering them), and computes the QK dot product, value aggregation and output projection in low dimension with standard PyTorch attention.

An illustrative walk-through (numbers are illustrative): head dimension 128, so 64 rotary pairs. Energies are computed on 32,768 calibration tokens, the top 32 pairs are kept, and the key dimension drops to 64; SVD also reduces values to 64. This layer's KV cache halves, QK and value-weighting compute each halve, and WQ​,WK​,WV​,WO​ shrink with them.

Optional recovery. For aggressive compression, the authors recommend one round of KL distillation on 1M FineWeb-Edu tokens, updating only the folded attention weights with everything else frozen. The effect is small at 75% retention and large at 50%, where it restores much of the lost quality. Both RACE and RAP get this step in the experiments for a fair comparison.

3. Core contribution

The main contribution is deciding which compressions are legal. The paper writes head-dimension compression as a matrix-product approximation with a RoPE constraint, then proves that among coordinate selections only "keep complete rotary pairs" can execute natively. With that result, the problem shifts from how to reconstruct to which legal pairs to keep, and the selection rule needs only forward statistics. In engineering terms, the compressed model is a standard Transformer with a smaller head dimension, and existing inference backends need no changes.

Compared with the concurrent RAP, the resulting structures look alike, both being low-dimensional attention that keeps complete rotary pairs, but the starting points differ. RAP treats it as structured parameter pruning and scores pairs with empirical Fisher from gradients of WK​, which needs backpropagation and a task loss, and Fisher estimates are sensitive to batch choice. RACE starts from matrix-product approximation, uses forward statistics only, and looks at both queries and keys. Appendix Figure 12 plots approximation error per layer; joint Q/K selection is lower than one-sided selection in most layers.

A few implementation details are worth copying. Calibration statistics and SVD run in FP32 and inference in BF16. Kept rotary pairs retain their original frequency indices and RoPE scaling rules rather than being renumbered as consecutive frequencies. The attention scale stays at the original 1/dk​​. System gains were measured on SDPA, eager and flash attention backends; wall-clock times differ by backend, but FLOPs are identical.

4. Experiments

Quality at matched KV budgets. The question is whether accuracy keeps up with reconstruction-based methods without reconstruction. Experiments on Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Granite-4.1-3B compare PALU, EigenAttention, Thin Keys and RAP on WikiText perplexity, mean accuracy over 15 zero-shot tasks, and mean score over 16 English LongBench tasks. At 75% KV retention, RACE keeps 98.5% (Qwen), 96.5% (Granite) and 97.5% (Llama) of the uncompressed zero-shot accuracy. Across compression levels its zero-shot and LongBench scores are close to PALU's without runtime reconstruction; it beats RAP at every level and has the lowest perplexity at 50% and 75% retention.

System benefits. The question is whether saved storage turns into lower compute and latency. Tests use Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct with 64K/128K/256K prompts and 128 output tokens, averaged over 10 runs. Gains grow with prompt length because attention takes a larger share of total cost. At 256K and 50% retention, prefill, decode and overall throughput improve 1.76×, 1.90× and 1.79×. Memory drops only 28.06% rather than half, because total memory also includes parameters and activations that RACE does not touch.

Stacking with token compression and quantization. Applying RACE at 75% and then H2O, SnapKV or KIVI at 50% each gives 37.5% total retention. Applying RACE to an already-compressed model costs more accuracy than applying it to the original, so the two compressions do not act independently. Stacking with KIVI quantization loses least, especially in perplexity.

Calibration data and selection criteria. Calibrating on in-distribution downstream data is at most 5.4% better than FineWeb-Edu, and most tasks differ by under 6%, so a generic corpus is good enough. In the ablation, the normalized product error is 0.1159 for Q-only, 0.1120 for K-only and 0.1051 for joint selection, and joint energy gives the best perplexity. It is also cheaper than magnitude, attention-product or gradient-Fisher scoring: calibration takes 2.717 s versus 11.616 s for attention-product and 8.290 s for Fisher, with peak memory of 6.142 GB versus 10.592 GB and 9.218 GB.

Wider heads compress better. The authors pretrained three ~245M-parameter GPT-NeoX-style models from scratch (4×256, 8×128 and 16×64 heads, 2.5B tokens), with similar uncompressed perplexity. Under aggressive compression the wide-head model holds up best, because wider heads offer more rotary pairs to choose from.

5. Limitations

The authors state that the energy criterion minimizes a separable upper bound, not the true error; 50% retention needs a distillation recovery step, so it is not fully training-free; and when stacked with token compression, the losses of the two stages compound. Theorem 1 covers only coordinate selection; to stay reconstruction-free the paper deliberately gives up more general dense low-rank projections, and it does not discuss whether a larger commuting family holds better solutions. In addition, system gains were measured on a single 3B model; gains below 256K are smaller, and the paper does not report what happens on large models or GQA architectures with very few KV heads.

6. What it means in practice

For long-context serving, RACE is easy to deploy: the output is a standard checkpoint with a smaller head dimension, backends such as vLLM or SGLang need no kernel changes, and it stacks with KV quantization like KIVI. A sensible pilot is 75% retention on your own model, calibrated on about 30K tokens of generic text with no distillation, then checking long-document tasks for regressions. Going to 50% calls for one KL distillation pass on the order of 1M tokens. If you are planning a new pretraining architecture and expect this kind of compression at deployment, fewer, wider heads leave more room for later structured dimension reduction.
