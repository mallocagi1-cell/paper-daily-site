Generative — Paper Daily, 2026-10-24

All 7 papers today tackle problems in deploying generative recommendation: how to build semantic IDs, how to generate a whole page, and how to cut inference cost. UniRec and CSG4Rec improve how semantic IDs are built, the first keeping popular codes from taking all exposure and the second putting collaborative signals into the IDs. Kuaishou's GLASS brings long-term behavior into generation, Netflix's GenPage generates homepage rows and titles one by one with a single model, and Kuaishou's OneLatent compresses reasoning before the SID into a few latent tokens. Two more papers study LLM-based conversational recommendation, one improving evaluation metrics and one letting the model decide each turn whether to ask or recommend.

4.65

1. UniRec: A Unified Expressive-Aligned Generative Recommendation Framework for E-commerce

RecSys 2026

Shopee ran an online A/B test on its home feed, giving the treatment and the control 20% of user traffic each, with the control served by the existing multi-stage retrieval and ranking system. The group served by UniRec placed 4.76% more orders and generated 5.60% more GMV. UniRec is a generative recommender that reads a user's profile and history, first generates the category hierarchy of a product, and then generates the short code sequence that names the exact product. Saying the category first tells the later steps what kind of product to recommend, which restores product information that the short codes compress away. Offline, it finds the target product among its top 50 candidates 22.6% more often than the strongest baseline, OneRec-V2. Generating the category before the product restores what code compression throws away.

2. OneLatent: Latent Reasoning for Efficient Foundation Recommendation Models

Kuaishou

Kuaishou ran a 7-day online A/B test on its local-services ads, with 5% of traffic in each group. The treatment, whose recommendations came from OneLatent, raised ad spend by 5.157%. A recommender that writes out its reasoning before naming an item recommends better, but writing text is too slow to serve online. The authors first have a teacher model write explanations covering what the user may need and whether a previously chosen item fits. Training has two steps. First, 3 new special tokens are placed after the user's behavior and the model learns to reproduce the teacher's explanation after them; then the target is switched from the explanation to the next item's ID. The 3 tokens thus learn to carry the reasoning, and in production the model generates only them before outputting the item ID. Its throughput is 17.49 times that of reasoning in text, about as fast as direct prediction, and its offline retrieval score is 17.44% higher than the version that writes its reasoning. Reasoning can stay inside the model instead of being written out as text every time.

3. GenPage: Towards End-to-End Generative Homepage Construction at Netflix

RecSys 2026Netflix

A Netflix member watched 50 minutes of Orange Is the New Black 30 days ago. Writing that record as text for GPT-5 takes 16 tokens, while the authors' format needs only 4, namely the title ID, the action type, a time bucket and a duration bucket. GenPage compresses a member's viewing history, profile and request context into such short tokens and feeds them to a single Transformer that generates the whole homepage, including which rows appear and which titles go in each row. In training it first imitates production homepages that got positive user feedback, then learns to estimate a value for each row or title it might place next, meaning whether the member would respond positively to it. At every generation step it masks options that break product rules, such as showing the same title twice or putting a non-comedy in a comedy row. In a 14-day online A/B test, five versions all significantly raised the core engagement metric Netflix uses for launch decisions, which the paper does not spell out. The best one raised it by 0.24% while cutting end-to-end serving latency by 20%. One generative model can lay out the whole homepage in one pass, and faster than the multi-stage system it replaces.

4. Coarse-to-Fine Long-term Interest Modeling for Generative Recommendation

RecSys 2026

On a short-video platform, the authors ran a two-week online A/B test with about 12 million users, 3% of all users, in the treatment group. The group served by GLASS had recall 3 percentage points higher than before. The difficulty is a user's old behavior: there is too much of it for a generative recommender to read in full, and reading only recent behavior loses long-term interests. GLASS gives each video a sequence of codes in which the first code names a broad category and later codes narrow down to the exact video. Each category is represented by the average vector of its videos. The model compares every video in the user's old history with each category, counts how many fall into each band of similarity, and uses that interest table to pick the first code. Once the category is fixed, it retrieves the user's past behavior in that category and uses it with recent behavior to choose the remaining codes. Offline on the Taobao multimodal dataset, its NDCG@3 is 29.91% higher than the strongest baseline. Long-term interests need not be read in full; pick the category first, then fetch the history that belongs to it.

5. CSG4Rec: Collaborative-Similarity Guided Semantic IDs Tokenization for Generative Sequential Recommendation

Generative recommenders often encode each product as a sequence of semantic IDs, and if the IDs come only from product text and images, two products with similar content but entirely different buyers end up with similar IDs. Existing methods learn content and behavior representations separately and align them afterwards, whereas CSG4Rec guides the IDs with behavioral similarity between products while building them, meaning whether the same users buy or click them, so such products get closer IDs; the abstract gives no formula. It also gives each user a sequence of IDs that represents their preferences and feeds it, together with the product IDs, to generative sequential recommendation. The paper appears in the journal Information Fusion, whose full text sits behind a captcha, so we read only the abstract, which reports no experimental numbers. Product IDs should reflect both content and who buys it together, or generative recommendation is misled by look-alike content.

6. Evaluating LLM-based conversational recommenders with semantic and genre-aware metrics

On ReDial and INSPIRED, two movie-recommendation dialogue datasets, LLM-driven conversational recommenders speak fluently, but metrics based on word overlap cannot tell whether the recommended movies suit the user. This study builds a full pipeline that first understands what the user says, then manages the dialogue, reasons over a knowledge graph and finally generates the reply with an LLM. On top of it, the authors add genre-based measures that check whether the recommended genres match the preferences the user stated, though the abstract does not say how they are scored. They also analyze how model size, knowledge-graph grounding and context length affect reply quality and hallucination. The journal's full text sits behind a captcha, so we read only the abstract, which gives no numbers. Conversational recommenders should be judged on whether the suggestions fit the user, not only on how natural they sound.

7. Integrating implicit desire fusion for proactive conversational recommendation systems with LLMs

On LastFM music recommendation, the authors let GPT-3.5-Turbo talk with a simulated user and check whether it recommends successfully within 15 turns. Without the step that infers what the user really wants, the success rate is 0.327, and with it the rate is 0.583. The paper's example is a user who first asks for calm music, then adds no fast tempo, and has a history of jazz and ambient music. DESIRE has the LLM put these clues together with retrieved song information, such as a jazz track with a slow tempo, and the songs the user just rejected, and infer what the user really wants. It then decides whether to keep asking or to recommend, picks a questioning or persuasion strategy, and every song attribute it mentions must be backed by the retrieved information. The key to conversational recommendation is piecing together the real need from scattered remarks, then deciding whether to ask or to recommend.
