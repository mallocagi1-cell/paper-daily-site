1. A Framework for Value-Aligned Modeling and Robust Experimentation

RecSys 2026

Google Discover's ads team found that the offline loss of its conversion model barely tracks online advertiser value, with a correlation of only 0.1, so models that improve offline often fail to win online. Advertiser value here is the number of conversions per click times the advertiser's target cost per conversion (tCPA), summed up. The authors weight each click in the loss by tCPA and pair every low-traffic experiment with a retrained production model on the same traffic as a control. With the weighting, the correlation between offline and online metrics rises to 0.65. Weighted by bids, the offline loss measures the conversions advertisers actually pay for.

2. scikit-rec: A Unified, Extensible Recommendation Library with scikit-rec-agent, a Conversational Interface

RecSys 2026Intuit

Recommendation teams often use a separate library for ranking, contextual bandits, uplift modeling and sequential recommendation, each with its own offline evaluation interface. Intuit's open-source scikit-rec splits business logic, scoring and the underlying model into three layers, so five kinds of recommenders share one evaluation interface. It ships six off-policy estimators, including IPS, SNIPS and DR, whose results match the Open Bandit Pipeline within floating-point error. Its internal version has run in several TurboTax and QuickBooks use cases for more than three years. On the same logs, bandit policies and ranking models can be compared directly with the same estimators.

3. Generalized Position-Based Model: Rethinking Position Weights in Ranking Off-Policy Evaluation

RecSys 2026

Off-policy evaluation (OPE) estimates how a new ranking policy would perform from the logs of an old one. Common estimators either importance-weight each position separately, which has high variance, or assume click probability equals relevance times the chance the position is seen, which is biased when position bias is misestimated. When estimating an item's effect at one position, INTERPOL also borrows its logged clicks at nearby positions, but the window size is set by hand and every position in it gets the same weight. Amazon Music's GPBM learns one weight for each pair of target and logged positions. Since the true error cannot be computed, it estimates two parts. Variance comes from how much the estimate moves across many small resamples of the log, and bias is bounded by how wrong the position-bias curve might be. The weights minimize their sum. Learning the weights from data removes the need to tune a window by hand
