1. UniPinRec: Unifying Generative Retrieval and Ranking at Pinterest Scale

RecSys 2026Pinterest

Pinterest trains and deploys its retrieval and ranking models separately, so the same user history is encoded twice online. UniPinRec merges both into one Transformer: it first encodes the user history and sends the resulting user vector to ANN retrieval; the retrieved candidate Pins are then appended after the history, and the same model outputs probabilities for actions such as save, click and hide, with no attention between candidates. The history is encoded only once, and its KV cache is reused directly by the ranking step. Offline Hit@3 is 14.7% higher than the production ranker; after replacing the old retrieval on the notifications surface, push opens from dormant users rose 1.72%.

2. UniRec: A Unified Expressive-Aligned Generative Recommendation Framework for E-commerce

RecSys 2026

The Shopee authors measured three-level semantic IDs (SIDs) built by standard RQ-KMeans: the top 10% most-exposed full codes took 89.62% of exposure, so a generative recommender's probability mass concentrates on a few popular codes. UniRec has the model first generate the item's level-2 and level-3 category tokens and only then the three SID levels, and when building SIDs it gives every code an exposure-weighted capacity limit. Once the category is generated, each later SID level only has to choose within that category. Offline HR@50 is 22.6% higher (relative) than OneRec-V2; online, against the old multi-stage pipeline, orders rose 4.76% and GMV 5.60%.

3. A Framework for Value-Aligned Modeling and Robust Experimentation

RecSys 2026

Google's Discover Ads team found that the offline Poisson Loss of its conversion model (pConvs) correlated only 0.1 with online advertiser value (Delivered Value: conversions per click times the advertiser's target cost per acquisition, tCPA, summed over clicks), so models that improved offline often did not gain once launched. The authors weight each click in the loss by its tCPA, and pair every low-traffic experiment with a retrained production model serving at the same traffic as its control. With tCPA weighting, the offline loss measures exactly the conversions advertisers are willing to pay for. Offline-to-online correlation rose to 0.65, and low-to-high-traffic experiment correlation rose from 0.53 to 0.93.

4. GenPage: Towards End-to-End Generative Homepage Construction at Netflix

RecSys 2026Netflix

The Netflix homepage consists of rows, each with a theme and a set of titles, and it used to be assembled by several stages such as a row model and an entity model. GenPage encodes the user's viewing history, device, time of day and so on as tokens forming a prompt, and a ~200M-parameter decoder-only Transformer generates row and title tokens one by one, left to right and top to bottom. Every row and every title is a single token, so business rules can be written directly as a candidate mask at each step. In an online A/B test the core metric used for launch decisions rose 0.24% and end-to-end latency fell 20%; offline, enriching the prompt reduced the loss more than scaling the model from 120M to 900M parameters.

5. Coarse-to-Fine Long-term Interest Modeling for Generative Recommendation

RecSys 2026

Because attention cost grows with the square of sequence length, generative recommenders usually consume only short behavior sequences. GLASS, from Kuaishou and Tsinghua, uses the long-term history in two places: before generating the first SID level, it computes the cosine similarity between every long-term history item and the center of each first-level code, counts the items per similarity tier, and compresses this into one interest token fed to the encoder; after the first SID level is generated, it uses that code to pull long-term history items sharing the same first-level code and fuses them through a gate to help generate the remaining two levels. The first-level code fixes the broad category, and the later two levels must tell items apart within it, so what they need is same-category history. In a two-week Kuaishou short-video A/B test, app usage time per user rose 0.052%.

6. scikit-rec: A Unified, Extensible Recommendation Library with scikit-rec-agent, a Conversational Interface

RecSys 2026Intuit

Recommendation teams often use a different library for ranking, contextual bandits, uplift and sequential recommendation, each with its own offline evaluation interface. Intuit's open-source scikit-rec splits business logic, scoring strategy and the underlying model into three layers (Recommender, Scorer, Estimator), and five recommendation paradigms share one evaluate() interface, which includes six off-policy evaluators such as IPS, SNIPS and DR whose results match Open Bandit Pipeline within floating-point error. On the same logged data, a bandit policy and a ranking model can be compared directly with the same evaluators. An internal version has powered 15 use cases in TurboTax and QuickBooks for more than three years under a sub-100 ms latency requirement.

7. SAGE: Sequence-level Adaptive Gradient Evolution for Generative Recommendation

RecSys 2026

OneRec-V2 uses GBPO for reinforcement-learning post-training. GBPO caps the importance ratio of every update at 1 and applies the same bound to positive and negative samples. Cold-start items start with low generation probability and get little positive feedback, so under this cap their probability can barely rise. SAGE computes the importance ratio as a geometric mean over the whole recommended list, raises the ratio cap above 1 for lists that received positive feedback, and penalizes more heavily the rejected lists whose category entropy is below the historical average. Only with separate bounds for positive and negative samples can scarce positive feedback push up the probability of new items. On Amazon Beauty, cold-start Recall@10 rises from GBPO's 0.326 to 0.619.

8. Generalized Position-Based Model: Rethinking Position Weights in Ranking Off-Policy Evaluation

RecSys 2026

Off-policy evaluation (OPE) uses an old policy's logs to estimate how a new ranking policy would perform. Common estimators either importance-weight each position separately, which has high variance, or assume click probability equals item relevance times the probability that the position is seen, which becomes biased when the position-bias curve is misestimated. INTERPOL compromises between the two with windows, but the windows must be set by hand and all positions inside a window are weighted equally. Amazon Music's GPBM learns a weight for every pair of logging position and target position, by minimizing an approximate upper bound on the estimation error. Because the weights are learned from data, the window size no longer has to be tuned by hand. In semi-synthetic experiments on Yahoo (29,921 queries) and MSLR, GPBM has lower MSE than all baselines in most settings.

9. A Control Function Framework for Mitigating Position Bias in Learning to Rank Systems

RecSys 2026

When a ranking model is trained on clicks, items ranked higher get more clicks, partly just because of their position. CFC borrows the control-function method from econometrics: the first stage fits the rank assigned by the historical ranker from query-item features, and the part of the rank the features cannot explain is the residual; the second stage adds the residual and its interactions with the features as extra inputs to the click model during training, and prediction uses only the learned feature part. It neither estimates the probability that each position is seen nor changes the ranker's loss, so LambdaMART and DNN rankers can both use it directly. On three benchmarks (Yahoo, MSLR-WEB10K, Istella-S) and the Baidu search log dataset Baidu-ULTR, CFC beats debiasing methods such as DLA and UPE on both ERR@10 and NDCG@10.

10. Generating Personalized Images for Sparse-Interaction Users with Uncertainty-Aware Retrieval and Dense Knowledge Guidance

RecSys 2026

Personalized image generation usually injects features of images the user has interacted with into a diffusion model. When a user has only 1–3 interactions, those images may be semantically unrelated to the target item, and injecting them brings in irrelevant styles. UDSIG first finds a batch of candidate images semantically close to the target item, then uses a pretrained recommender to score this user's preference for each candidate and picks the high scorers as references; separately, it trains a reward model on active users' dense interactions to guide generation for sparse users. Because references are picked from the whole catalog, they are not limited to the user's own few interactions. On three datasets (POG, SER, MovieLens), in a human evaluation with 50 participants, personalization ranking improved by up to 25.12%.
