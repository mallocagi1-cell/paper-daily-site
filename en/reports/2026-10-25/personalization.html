Personalization — Paper Daily, 2026-10-25

Today's personalization theme is making use of what users actually saw. LinkedIn puts people who were shown but not opened into its training objective, a carousel bandit updates only on positions that were really browsed, and Kuaishou has several agents write ad scripts per user. Other papers handle recommendation under hard constraints, including a shared household fridge, medication sets and ad auctions inside assistants.

1. PAIR: Preference-Aligned Intent Retrieval for Premium People Recommendation at LinkedIn

LinkedIn

When LinkedIn recommends people for Premium members to browse, whom a member has already been shown is itself a preference signal, but neither the general member embedding nor an off-the-shelf LLM embedding has learned it. PAIR treats people who were shown and opened as positives and people who were shown but skipped as negatives, and trains a small 0.6B model contrastively so that exposure enters the training objective directly. Offline MAR@100 rises from 0.288 with the general member embedding to 0.450, and an online A/B test raises click-through by 7.5% with no notable change in serving latency. Once the objective knows who was shown but skipped, a small model beats a large general member embedding.

2. Ranking Bandits for Carousel Interfaces with Observable Browsing Depth

In carousels and feeds, users usually stop at some depth, and the positions below it are never seen. The classic position-based model (PBM) still treats those positions as seen but not clicked, and counts them as negative feedback. This paper assumes browsing depth can be observed and proposes three algorithms, OD-UCB, OD-TS and OD-DMED, which update their statistics only with positions the user actually saw; it also proves that the regret of OD-UCB grows logarithmically. In simulated shallow and deep browsing environments, OD-TS matches PBM-TS, which is told how likely each position is to be seen, and all three new methods beat PBM-UCB. If you can see how far a user browsed, unseen positions should not count as negative feedback.

3. Bezier-Guided Adaptive Flow for Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation

Cross-domain sequential recommendation usually aligns preferences learned in one domain with another as a whole, but users differ a lot in how consistent their interests are across the two domains, so one global alignment also carries over irrelevant preferences. BGAFlow first builds a semantic space shared by both domains, then moves each user's representation step by step toward the target domain along a reference path given by a Bezier curve. A per-user gate decides how much to transfer: users whose interests agree across domains transfer more, and users whose interests differ transfer less. How much preference to transfer should be decided per user, by how consistent that person's interests are across domains.

4. Behavior lifetime modeling uses time sensitive behavioral signals to rank candidate items for purchase in E commerce recommendation

JJiangxi University of Software Professional Technology

In multi-behavior recommendation, the value of a view, a favorite or an add-to-cart for predicting a purchase fades over time, so an add-to-cart from long ago should no longer count as strong purchase intent. BLMRec encodes each behavior by its type and by how long before the prediction time it happened, estimates its short-term and long-term effect with several time windows, and feeds these freshness signals into purchase ranking. On the Taobao dataset its Recall@20 is 6.0% higher than the strongest baseline. Each kind of behavior has a shelf life, and ranking should first check whether it has expired.

5. MAPS: A Multi-Agent Personalized Script-Driven Video Advertising System Enhanced by Reinforcement Learning

Kuaishou

Video ads are usually produced in batches and then matched to audiences by retrieval, so one ad has to serve very different users. Kuaishou's MAPS has four cooperating agents that build the user profile, write the script, render the video and feed delivery results back, and the script-writing agent is trained with reinforcement learning to write a different script for each user. In online experiments, compared with a generative baseline, click-through rises by 2.87% and average revenue per user (ARPU) by 9.85%. When creatives can only be made in advance, writing the ad script per user is more direct than improving creative retrieval.

6. UserArena: Benchmarking Interactive User Simulation for Long-Horizon Shopping Agents

Agent-based shopping recommendation needs an environment that can simulate long user interactions for offline evaluation, but existing data rarely contains fine-grained real user trajectories, and offline results are hard to match with online A/B tests. UserArena releases 64,729 session trajectories from 11,010 real users, together with an interactive environment and an evaluation that scores whole trajectories. Its offline policy evaluation can be checked against production A/B results, which tests whether a simulated user can be trusted. Simulated users can be trusted for evaluation only when long real trajectories and online results are there to check them.

7. FairFRESH: A Neuro-Symbolic Framework for Fair and Explainable Household Food Recommendation

A family shares one fridge, but each member has different dietary rules, so recommending food to each person separately can make them compete for the same item or break someone's dietary restriction. FairFRESH treats household food recommendation as a constrained fair-allocation problem. It first learns each member's food preferences, then allocates by the welfare of the whole household, and finally uses a deterministic rule filter so that no dietary rule is broken, with Shapley values explaining each recommendation. Compared with recommending to each person and then resolving conflicts, inequality among members (the Gini coefficient) falls by 42%, and every recommendation satisfies the dietary rules. Shared stock and dietary restrictions are hard constraints and need rules that can be enforced.

8. Truthful by Omission: Click Thinning for LLM-Native Advertising

When a generative assistant places ads inside its answer, an ad's click-through rate depends on the passage that hosts it, and users often stop after the first click, so earlier ads take clicks from later ones. Here, existing auction mechanisms either guarantee welfare but cannot ensure advertisers bid truthfully, or ensure truthful bidding while their welfare gets worse as the number of passages grows. The authors propose click thinning, which omits some ads at random and shows earlier ads less often, so each ad's overall click probability no longer depends on the order, and then charges with VCG-style payments. Truthful bidding becomes the best strategy, and welfare is at least half of the optimum. Ads inside an assistant cannot reuse search position auctions; truthfulness and welfare have to be redesigned together.

9. From Scores to Safe Sets: Exact Safety--Constrained Decoding for Medication Recommendation

Generative medication recommendation outputs a set of drugs, and drug-drug interaction constraints apply to the whole set, yet the usual approach predicts drugs one by one and then deletes conflicting ones to repair the set. The authors fix the FLAME model's scores, prescription size and interaction constraints on MIMIC-III, and compare increasingly strong local repair methods with exact global decoding by mixed integer programming. Local repair itself introduces avoidable errors, while global decoding directly picks the highest-scoring drug set that satisfies the constraints, and the advantage grows as the constraints get stricter. When a constraint applies to the whole set, solve for the whole set instead of deleting drugs afterwards.

10. FAIRGate: Agentic Runtime Policy Selection and Verified Repair for Fairer Recommendation Delivery

Fair recommendation usually fixes one fairness objective at training time, or applies a fixed re-ranking rule after scoring, but each online request has a different candidate pool, exposure debt and utility budget, so the right action differs too. FAIRGate adds a policy-selecting agent between the base ranker and the final top-K, which picks one policy per request from a library of pre-verified policies, such as boosting long-tail exposure or balancing exposure across groups. An executor then applies the policy, and a validator checks the result and repairs it if it falls short. Fairness in production should be picked from verified policies, not redefined on the fly for every request.
