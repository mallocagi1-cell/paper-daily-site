1. Keep it Simple! A Multiple-Choice Task can Benefit Generative Recommendation

📖 Deep dive →

Generative recommenders often predict the target item's SID directly and never explicitly learn negatives or relative ranking, which sits poorly with the idea behind BPR, where the rank of a positive against negatives matters. Adding a simple multiple-choice auxiliary task to the learning objective, and synthesizing its training data with task identifiers and candidate construction, supplies that comparison signal with only a small change.

2. CARE: Contrastive-Anchored Residual Encoding with Listwise Reconstruction for Retrieval-Oriented Semantic IDs

📖 Deep dive →

Generative retrieval produces a document's SID, so where the tokenizer puts quantization error directly limits what can still be recalled. Common SID methods quantize residual codes on document embeddings alone and do not check whether a query can still find its relevant documents after quantization.

1. TokenFormer V2: Breaking the Resolution-Complexity Dilemma in Unified Recommendation

Tencent📖 Deep dive →

Industrial recommenders need fine-grained long behavior histories, yet they must stay inside a strict serving budget: keeping every event is expensive, and compact summaries throw evidence away. This work decouples representation resolution from interaction density. It encodes behaviors in local groups, expands range through hierarchical merging and global synchronization, and still lets the final predictor read every encoded event directly.

2. CLEAR: Legible and Steerable Recommendation via Grounded Natural-Language Interests

Meta📖 Deep dive →

Recommenders often bury user taste in hidden vectors that people cannot inspect or change, so adding or suppressing an interest usually has no handle. CLEAR, deployed at Meta, adds an editable natural-language interest layer: each interest is grounded in the user's own history and serves as an address for retrieval, ranking, and exposure control.

2. EchoSpec: Training Search Agents to Weigh Evidence Above Echoes

Tsinghua University📖 Deep dive →

In real corpora, one claim is copied and paraphrased across many pages, while the primary source that settles a question often appears once. Search agents trained with reinforcement learning treat those echoes as if they were independent sources: eight paraphrased false-claim echoes overturn 58% to 73% of previously correct answers.

LEAD STORY · AAi2

1. Supercharging Olmo-core for Efficient and Scalable MoE Training

AAi2

Mixture-of-experts models add parameters, but the useful compute per token barely changes. If the training stack still moves and stores parameters as a dense model would, throughput falls as the expert pool grows. Olmo-core replaces fully sharded data parallelism with distributed data parallelism, and adds expert parallelism, pipeline parallelism, a distributed optimizer, and activation rematerialization. On B300 eight-GPU nodes, with up to 512 GPUs, a 1.2-trillion-parameter run that uses MXFP8 (a microscaling 8-bit float format) and per-layer rematerialization reaches 858 useful-model TFLOPS per GPU per second. The authors also find that overlapping communication with computation can slow the overlapped kernels by more than it hides. Once experts multiply, the first bottleneck is moving parameters, not the math per step. For teams training very large MoE models, parallelism, precision, and rematerialization have to be designed together, or useful compute is spent on traffic first.

2. Efficient Distributed MLLM Training with Cornstarch

ICML 2026

Multimodal models stitch vision or speech encoders together with a language model, so both the architecture and the data are uneven. Dropping them into a language-model training stack often leaves pipeline stages and context-parallel ranks badly balanced. When an encoder is frozen, a pipeline cut that assumed every module was trainable falls out of balance; tokens of different types also attend over different ranges,. Cornstarch splits the pipeline with frozen modules in mind, and assigns tokens in context parallelism by attention work. On 24 A40 GPUs, end-to-end training throughput rises to 2.26 times that of existing systems on average; after the frozen state is built into the cut, an iteration can be up to 2.46 times faster. The bottleneck in multimodal training is often idle time between unlike modules and unlike data. For teams training vision-language models, the parallel cut has to see frozen weights and attention masks, rather than reuse a text-only plan.

4.70

1. Ego2Sim2Humanoid: Agentic Real-to-Sim for Learning Loco-Manipulation VLAs without Robot Demonstrations

Egocentric glasses can record a person's demonstration without occupying a robot, but a person's stature and hand shape differ from the robot's, so copying the hand motion often misses the contact or fails to hold. This method reconstructs the scene from the glasses views and head trajectory, plans stances and grasps the robot can reach without penetrating the object, then completes whole-body motion in a physics simulator and renders training views from the robot camera. Fine-tuned only on this converted data, with no real-robot demonstrations of the target tasks, a whole-body VLA on a Unitree G1 lifts the bottle in nine of twenty trials (45%) and places the hat on the head in five of twenty (25%). Turn a human recording into a physically feasible robot trajectory first, then fine-tune. For teams collecting loco-manipulation data for humanoids, recording can use only glasses and need not occupy the robot.

4.62

2. ResWAM: Learning What Changes, Not What Stays, for World-Action Models

Michigan State UniversityCiscoUniversity of MichiganIBM

Adding a future prediction to an action policy is meant to help the model anticipate how its actions will change the world. Over an action chunk, though, the image often barely moves, so a loss on the complete future is dominated by the static background. This work supervises only the residual from the current frame to a future frame in a frozen visual feature space, and that residual concentrates on the arm and the object being manipulated. The resulting residual world-action model has a few billion parameters and uses no pretraining on robot trajectories beyond the benchmark demonstrations. On real-robot Open, Put and Stack it reaches 80.0% average success, ten percentage points above the strongest baseline, at 92.7 milliseconds per action chunk. Learn what is changing in the image, rather than copying the unmoving background again. For closed-loop use on hardware, compute can be spent on change, without carrying a heavy video generation model.
