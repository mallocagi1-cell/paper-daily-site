1. Needwise: Generation-Free Conversational Recommendation from Need-Level Typed Decisions

Adobe

In conversational recommendation, users add, drop, withdraw, and reverse needs, while catalogs record only some of the attributes. Typical LLM rankers reread the whole dialogue at every turn, which is slow and does not show which needs an item actually meets. The new system maps each turn onto typed decisions over fixed options, then ranks must-first by item type, place, and price. On Google Local, Needwise-HF reaches 0.383 nDCG@10, ahead of GPT-6 Sol at 0.343. Its median response is 2.5 seconds, compared with 5.6 seconds for the latter. The typed state keeps the ranking across topic switches, but it still recognizes only a limited share of withdrawn needs.

2. SARA: An Auditor-in-the-Loop LLM Recommender for Sustainability Reports

FFraunhofer IAISLamarr InstitutePPwCUniversity of Bonn

Auditing sustainability reports means mapping a checklist drawn from about 1,700 regulatory requirements onto long, repeatedly revised drafts, then judging compliance. The new system is a Word add-in that, for each requirement, recommends the relevant passages and predicts a compliance verdict, while the auditor stays in control. It first predicts a topic and sends only passages from that sub-topic to the language model. On two expert-annotated reports, that is about six times cheaper than feeding the full report to the model, cuts runtime by 42%, and raises precision and F1 by 4.4 and 3.1 points. It is already in production in PwC Germany's sustainability-assurance practice.

1. Weight Coding: Evolving Web-Scale Recommenders through Direct Weight Edits with LLM Agents

Pinterest

Web-scale recommenders usually have to retrain or fine-tune to change behavior, which is expensive and makes it hard to steer exposure for a specific kind of content. Pinterest has an LLM agent edit production weights directly and keep or discard each change with forward evaluation, without backpropagation. On the 147-million-parameter Image Search two-tower retriever, a few edits raised Recall@10 by up to 15 percentage points on evaluation data not used to invent the edits, using about a quarter of the GPU hours of a full post-training fine-tune. In online A/B tests, saves per user from the edited retrieval source rose 8.4% to 10.7%, and the same process can steer exposure toward freshness or products.

2. From Head to Tail: LLM-Augmented Sequential Recommendation and Trade-off Optimization

CIKM 2026 WorkshopComcastCMU

Recommenders often spend exposure on popular items, so long-tail and cold-start items with few interactions are hard to rank well. This work partitions items by interaction frequency, uses an LLM to generate synthetic interactions, and boosts each group's ranking quality and coverage while keeping the drop in overall NDCG@20 within a bound. The best augmentation for each group is not simply additive. On an industrial dataset with a SASRec backbone, group NDCG@20 more than doubled for extreme long-tail items and rose 60% for regular long-tail items; on MovieLens-32M the same two groups improved by 40% and 25%, without a large drop in the overall metric.

1. PROSPER: LLMs as Sparse Retrievers for First-Stage Product Search

WSDM 2027Chinese Academy of SciencesUUniversity of Chinese Academy of SciencesUniversity of AmsterdamAlibaba

First-stage product search has to reach high recall and still be easy to deploy. Sparse retrieval is interpretable and practical, but short queries and titles often miss each other in the vocabulary; using an LLM as a sparse retriever can also wash out literal signals such as brand names and model identifiers. The new method adds a residual to restore underweighted literal terms and uses a top-k window to sparsify the representation early. On the Multi-CPR e-commerce set, Hit@1000 is 10.2 points above BM25 and also ahead of SPLADE-v2. In a large-scale online A/B test, net GMV rose 1.60%, showing a revenue gain for an industrial hybrid retriever.

2. SupportChain: Trace-Calibrated Aggregation of Quote-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Multi-Hop RAG with Small Readers

UUNC Chapel Hill

Strong multi-hop retrieval QA often comes from graph indexes read by very large models, and building such an index means running a language model over the whole corpus. The new method splits a question into hops, binds each hop's answer to a sentence quoted from a retrieved passage, estimates reliability from the execution trace, and merges runs by the support of their answers and the pooled reliability of those that agree. With a smaller 9B reader it reaches 63.0 F1 on MuSiQue, above the previous best of 59.2 from a 20B reader. With the same reader and embedder, it is significantly better than HippoRAG 2 on all five datasets, by up to 17.2 F1, and it builds no index.

1. SignalCount: Approximate Counts with Structured and Semantic Predicates in Seconds

Applications often need a count of how many filtered records meet a criterion that only a reader of the text can judge, such as why a patient stopped a drug, and they need that count in seconds before starting a batch pipeline. Prior systems have an expensive model label a sample at query time, so every query waits. The new system calibrates a fast classifier offline from logs of the production judge and does not call that judge at query time. It samples at most a thousand records, then multiplies the average calibrated probability by the exact filtered count. In a multi-tenant production deployment, p99 latency is 3.8 seconds; on tenants held out from fitting, the interval covers the pipeline count in 92% of estimates, though the estimates run about 15% high in aggregate.

2. Fast 4-bit KV-Cache Decoding on Hopper GPUs: Why Existing Kernels Are Slow and How to Fix Them

A four-bit KV cache reads about half the bytes of FP8, but Hopper GPUs have no FP4 hardware, so existing kernels have to decode the values in software. On H200, vLLM's NVFP4 path is up to 1.7 times slower than FP8 and uses only a small fraction of memory bandwidth. The new decode kernel overlaps loads with compute and lays the cache out at write time so decoded values can enter the tensor cores from registers. Inside vLLM, on a mid-size Llama 3.1 model at moderate batches, it decodes 1.12 to 1.64 times faster per step than FlashInfer's FP8 path and holds substantially more tokens. On five mid-size models, repeated runs show no significant accuracy difference from BF16 KV.

LEAD STORY · NVIDIA / HKUST (Guangzhou) / University of Cambridge

1. Tacinf: A Unified Tactile Infrastructure for Robot Manipulation

NVIDIAHKUST (Guangzhou)University of Cambridge

Force-aware manipulation needs robots to infer contact force from the task and from touch, but they often lack a low-cost, reusable data and evaluation stack. The new modular infrastructure ties together simulation synthesis, a tactile benchmark, vision-tactile-language-action policies, handheld collection with the same gripper used at deployment, and task-aligned sim-to-real evaluation, and a task instruction can also name the desired force. A foundation model fine-tuned only on tactile simulation data transfers zero-shot to different real-robot tasks. When object positions and instances are both randomized, mixing teleoperation, simulation, and handheld data raises mean real-robot success from 20% with teleoperation alone to 60%. Under firm versus gentle instructions, measured grip force also separates as requested.

2. Force2Act: A Force-Aware Visuomotor Policy for Generalizable Compliant Robot Manipulation

NUSZhejiang University

Contact-rich manipulation requires a robot to regulate the force it applies, not only where it moves. Policies that copy force from demonstrations fail once the interaction needs more force than any demonstration provided. The new method first plans motion and a relative interaction force for an action chunk, then a mixture of force experts regulates the contact at every control step so the hold is kept when contact changes inside the chunk. On a simulation benchmark of five contact-rich tasks, overall success is 93.8%, against 77.0% for the strongest baseline. On a real robot, average success over four tasks is 94.2%, while the baselines reach at most 27.5%.
