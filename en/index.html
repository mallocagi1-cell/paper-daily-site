4.15

1. TransX Technical Report: Scaling Sequence Transduction for Recommendation

TransX caches reusable behavior memory and separates sequence encoding from candidate-conditioned scoring. Offline prefill and nearline refresh keep history processing off the request path. Its base model raised CTR by 6.02% over the production baseline in an online experiment.

Key insight: Compute history when behavior changes, then reuse the state across candidates. This shifts serving work toward candidate discrimination, while introducing explicit obligations around freshness, version compatibility and recovery.

4.05

2. RecSoup: Efficient Model Merging Approach for Controllable Multi-Objective Recommendation

RecSoup makes accuracy–diversity trade-offs adjustable by reusing objective-specific checkpoints. It prunes task-vector updates and learns preference-dependent merge coefficients. Parameter construction was about 165–228 times faster than a hypernetwork across three benchmarks.

Key insight: Controllability can come from combining existing specialists. Pruning and coefficient learning solve different problems: preserving useful updates and choosing their mixture. The latency claim concerns parameter construction, not the full recommendation pipeline.

4.45

1. Gryphon-v2: One Model in Place of a Cascade - Generate-and-Rank Recommender with Rollout Distillation

Gryphon-v2 encodes user history once, generates semantic item IDs, and ranks the resolved items with a small shared-state head. A larger teacher supplies training targets but stays out of serving. Replacing the Yandex Music cascade increased active users by 1.41% in an online experiment.

Key insight: Distill on candidates the generator actually produces as well as logged impressions. This teaches the ranking head its serving distribution while retaining exposure-based supervision; sharing the history encoder then removes redundant online work.

3.95

2. Vela: User Behavior as Supervision Sparks LLM Intelligence

Vela turns real behavior histories into tasks and accepted training trajectories rather than treating logs only as next-click labels. Fine-tuning on this supervision improves user-related tasks by up to 11.5 points over the base model.

Key insight: Behavior logs can supervise preference and intent inference, not just item prediction. Task filtering and trajectory acceptance matter because an observed action alone does not provide a reliable reasoning trace.

3.61

1. Information-Gain Calibrated On-Policy Distillation for Deep Search Agents

The method calibrates on-policy distillation with information gained after retrieval, adjusting both turn-level supervision and query preferences. The 2B student gains 3.4 accuracy points on average across six multimodal search benchmarks.

Key insight: Train against what a query retrieves, rather than its appearance to the teacher. Token volume can underweight short but informative search turns.

3.40

2. Retrieved but Not Read: Understanding Why Search-Augmented LLMs Fail to Use Available Evidence

The study separates finding current evidence from using it in the final answer. Across six open-weight configurations and a proprietary deployment, models sometimes rely on snippets and retain stale answers despite available evidence.

Key insight: Track evidence discovery, page opening and final answer use separately. Better retrieval alone may leave a stale-answer failure unresolved.

4.25

1. SearchJev: A Fast and Calibrated System-1 Model for Search Agents

SearchJev directly scores legal search decisions and delegates uncertain cases to a reasoning model. The dual-system agents achieve a 3.7–4.7× speedup in active search time on BrowseComp-Plus.

Key insight: Separate short relevance and sufficiency decisions from planning and answer generation. Calibrated confidence controls delegation; track handoff frequency and end-to-end tail latency alongside decision accuracy.

4.15

2. Beyond Outcome Rewards: Constructing and Assigning Retrieval Credit for Search Agents

The paper couples intermediate retrieval supervision with action-local credit assignment. Under matched Qwen3-4B training conditions, evidence-coverage local credit raises average answer F1 by 3.09 points over outcome-only training.

Key insight: Design the retrieval signal and its credit location together. Evidence feedback is more useful when attached to the search action that produced it than when spread indiscriminately across a trajectory.

4.35

1. Push-Based KV Transfer to Improve TTFT and TPOT for Disaggregated Inference in vLLM

A push-based NIXL connector overlaps destination allocation with prefill and moves transfer setup off the decode loop. In the specified Qwen3-32B 1P1D test on EC2 P5en with EFA, TTFT improves by up to about 3× over pull mode.

Key insight: Transfer ownership and setup placement matter alongside bandwidth. Overlap preparation with prefill and keep coordination off the token-generation critical path.

4.10

2. Improve TTFT for Multi-Turn Agentic Workloads with Bi-Directional KV Transfer in vLLM

Bidirectional KV transfer lets prefill nodes reuse state produced on decode nodes in earlier turns. In the specified GLM-5-FP8 1P1D multi-turn benchmark, TTFT improves by up to 3.3×.

Key insight: Output-side cache becomes input-side state on the next turn. Session routing, retention and recompute controls determine whether that reuse survives in a deployed service.
