1. Stable and Granular Policy Optimization for Generative Recommendation

NeurIPS 2026

Generative recommenders decode sequential semantic IDs for items, and because core metrics are scored at the sequence level, reinforcement learning is a natural fit. Off-the-shelf group-based algorithms struggle here: token-level methods become much more unstable, while sequence-level methods weight every token equally and ignore their different roles. A group-based method built for this setting keeps a sequence-level ratio for stability while injecting token-wise advantages. It upweights high-confidence tokens on positive trajectories to reinforce successful retrieval, strictly penalizes them on negative ones to encourage exploration, and uses an adaptive scale to bound the variance of the reshaped advantages. On two public benchmarks and a billion-scale industrial dataset, it consistently beats six strong reinforcement-learning baselines. In an online test on a commercial platform, click-through rate rose 2.36% and advertising revenue rose 1.95%.

2. Better Rankers, Worse Retrievers: The Hidden Cost of RL Post-Training in Generative Recommendation

HKUSTTTrip.com📖 Deep dive →

Post-training generative recommenders with reinforcement learning after supervised fine-tuning is now standard, but gains are usually reported on the first ten results, while deployed models often hand hundreds of candidates to a downstream ranker. A systematic measurement shows that reinforcement learning improves accuracy at the very top, yet on all five datasets studied it lowers recall of the candidate list: the model becomes a better ranker and a worse retriever.

1. COTTI: Contrastive Training and Time-Aware Inference for Robust Industrial Recommendation under Promotional Drift

AlibabaUCASSanta Clara University📖 Deep dive →

Promotional events rewrite traffic mix, purchase behavior, and conversion probabilities across time horizons, so scoring with historical interest representations often fails when the event starts. A production recommender separates historical representation learning from event-time score adjustment: it aligns paired histories of the same user, down-weights promotional examples far from historical interest, and adjusts the output with a temporal event kernel while leaving the predictor frozen.

2. Lost in the Funnel: Selection-Induced Miscalibration in Ranking and Decision Systems

Microsoft📖 Deep dive →

Multi-stage ranking often treats a score calibrated at the model boundary as a probability through filtering, reranking, business rules, and auctions, yet calibration is usually checked only where the score is produced. An industry diagnosis finds that decision-time expected calibration error rises four- to fivefold, and the breakdown comes from selection rather than model error: even a well-calibrated score can break after later filters.

1. MIPR: A Benchmark for Reasoning-Intensive Multimodal Intent-to-Product Retrieval with Multi-Category Solutions

AlibabaTTongji UniversityEEastern Institute of Technology, Ningbo📖 Deep dive →

Real e-commerce queries often state an intent without naming a product, and the same intent may be fulfilled by items from several categories, yet most multimodal retrieval benchmarks assume a fairly well-defined target. A new intent-to-product benchmark provides 3,522 multimodal queries and a shared corpus of 30,731 products, with reasoning-intensive retrieval, multi-category solutions, and fine-grained relevance labels.

1. Efficient Wide-Beam Generative Retrieval with Model-Native Trie Decoding and Shared-Context Attention

LinkedIn📖 Deep dive →

Generative retrieval maps requests to catalog entries as short semantic IDs, and trie-constrained beam search keeps those IDs valid, but wide beams create two serving costs: vocabulary-wide score tensors that are mostly discarded, and repeated attention over the same prompt. A model-native output interface stores the identifier trie as validated GPU tensors, avoiding Python constraint callbacks and vocabulary-wide candidate buffers.

2. SIMQ: A Single Graph-based Index for Multi-Vector Query

TTongji UniversityAAnt GroupXXi’an Jiaotong University

Multi-vector retrievers represent documents as sets of token-level vectors and score them with MaxSim, which is effective but expensive to scale to large corpora. Existing approximations either materialize a full centroid interaction matrix at every query and rely on GPU clustering, or use data-oblivious compression whose recall is unstable and needs repeated compensation. A single-graph index clusters all token vectors with data-aware sampling, builds one graph over the centroids, and approximates MaxSim by per-token graph search, without materializing an interaction matrix or depending on GPU acceleration. On five benchmarks, at nearly matched recall, the data-aware partition offers comparable or higher query throughput; on SciDocs it reaches 121.6 queries per second, above 105.0 for k-means. The method has been integrated into VSAG, Ant Group's open-source vector search library, so multi-vector retrieval can scale mainly on CPU.

1. HandUMI: A Modular Hand-Worn Parallel-Jaw Interface for Robot-Free Bimanual Data Capture

MMu RoboticsMcGillMilaTUMUUPC

Bimanual demonstrations usually occupy a robot during collection, and the motions are hard to move to a new body. A hand-worn parallel-jaw interface uses swappable fingertips to match the robot gripper, tracks the hands with headset controllers, and measures aperture with encoders. Bimanual demonstrations can therefore be captured without a robot and then retargeted by inverse kinematics to seven simulated bimanual arms, with median position error of at most 1.2 millimeters. On real PiPER arms folding a towel, an ACT policy trained on these robot-free demonstrations succeeds in 12 of 20 attempts, while the same policy trained on only an earlier slice of the data succeeds in none. The hardware and software are open source, and a pair with a Quest 3S stays inexpensive. For labs that need more bimanual data, demonstrations need not be bound to the target robot first.

2. Continually Evolving Skill Knowledge in Vision Language Action Model

NeurIPS 2026Shanghai Jiao Tong UniversitySShanghai Innovation InstituteUniversity of CambridgeNTUMMIT SMARTAAgiBot

Vision-language-action models can accumulate knowledge in pretraining, but continual learning remains hard. Existing continual imitation methods often add parameters or modules, which does not scale well for large models. A knowledge-driven framework with no parameter growth jointly optimizes task representations and a self-evolving knowledge space, and routes experts using a knowledge posterior over relations and semantic embeddings for task specialization. It has two forms: one that is task-centric, and one that models reusable skills across tasks in a hierarchy. On LIBERO, the method stays strong against vision-language-action and continual-imitation baselines with only 1% replay, and dual-arm real-world experiments validate knowledge transfer across embodiments and scenes. The hierarchical variant is stronger at hierarchical manipulation, and visualizations show knowledge retention and task discovery. For robot models that must keep adding skills, a knowledge space can evolve without growing parameters to remember old tasks.
