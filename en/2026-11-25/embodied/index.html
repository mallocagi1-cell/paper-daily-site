Embodied AI — Paper Daily, 2026-11-25

Today's embodied papers take force, spatial relations, and evaluation throughput together. Some build a reusable stack for touch and force, and some plan force from the current load and hold contact when it changes. Whole-body motion priors coordinate walking and manipulation on a humanoid, while 3D relation graphs and spatial foresight supply geometry that vision-language-action models usually leave implicit. Safety filters are no longer only a test-time correction; training also prepares the policy for states after an intervention. Human play can teach skills without action labels, and large-scale physical evaluation starts using automatic task generation and grading to cut manual resets. Other work splits key-action reasoning from dense motion, or derives perceptual supervision from the manipulation process itself.

1. Tacinf: A Unified Tactile Infrastructure for Robot Manipulation

NVIDIAHKUST (Guangzhou)University of Cambridge

Force-aware manipulation needs robots to infer contact force from the task and from touch, but they often lack a low-cost, reusable data and evaluation stack. The new modular infrastructure ties together simulation synthesis, a tactile benchmark, vision-tactile-language-action policies, handheld collection with the same gripper used at deployment, and task-aligned sim-to-real evaluation, and a task instruction can also name the desired force. A foundation model fine-tuned only on tactile simulation data transfers zero-shot to different real-robot tasks. When object positions and instances are both randomized, mixing teleoperation, simulation, and handheld data raises mean real-robot success from 20% with teleoperation alone to 60%. Under firm versus gentle instructions, measured grip force also separates as requested.

2. Force2Act: A Force-Aware Visuomotor Policy for Generalizable Compliant Robot Manipulation

NUSZhejiang University

Contact-rich manipulation requires a robot to regulate the force it applies, not only where it moves. Policies that copy force from demonstrations fail once the interaction needs more force than any demonstration provided. The new method first plans motion and a relative interaction force for an action chunk, then a mixture of force experts regulates the contact at every control step so the hold is kept when contact changes inside the chunk. On a simulation benchmark of five contact-rich tasks, overall success is 93.8%, against 77.0% for the strongest baseline. On a real robot, average success over four tasks is 94.2%, while the baselines reach at most 27.5%.

3. CHAMP: Coordinated Humanoid Loco-Manipulation with Whole-Body Motion Priors

DD-RoboticsCCAS Institute of Automation

A humanoid that walks and uses its hands in human environments has to learn motion priors from messy data and then use them for coordinated control. The new method pretrains a vision-language model on about two thousand hours of human and robot data to predict future body and hand motion, then a coordination-oriented action expert sparsely selects, transforms, and fuses features from several layers to produce body and hand actions. Overall success is 84.8% on seven simulated tasks and 91.4% on seven real-world tasks with a Unitree G1, ahead of the evaluated baselines in both settings. On the long-horizon popcorn-serving task, success is 70.0%, compared with 40.0% for the strongest baseline.

4. WeaveVLA: 3D Relational Grounding for Vision-Language-Action Models

HKUSTHHong Kong Polytechnic UniversityXidian UniversityZhejiang University

Vision-language-action models can follow instructions, but their grasp of task-relevant 3D relations, and of how those relations change during manipulation, is usually only implicit. The new method uses a task-conditioned sparse graph to encode relative 3D relations among objects, the gripper, and nearby references, keeps a recurrent edge state across observations, and reads the graph into relational tokens that condition the action expert. On two backbones, average success on LIBERO is over 98%. On three real-world tasks, the StarVLA-GR00T variant raises average success from 55.6% to 70.0% under standard conditions, and it also improves under out-of-distribution perturbations.

5. Intervention-Robust VLA: Diverse Demonstrations for Robot Deployment with Safety Filters

Technical University of MunichUniversity of Utah

A test-time safety filter can correct unsafe actions, but it can also drive the robot into states the training data barely covered, and task success then falls. Learning safe behavior directly from data would require covering many safety-critical situations, which is often impractical. The new method adds diverse task-completing demonstrations under the same data budget, broadening task-relevant state coverage so training better matches safety-filtered deployment. In real-world in-distribution and out-of-distribution tests, it stays safe and raises the safe success rate of nominal policies from 0% to as high as 90%. On both evaluated vision-language-action models, it consistently outperforms training on nominal data alone.

6. Foresight Forcing: Learning Spatial Foresight for Robotic Manipulation

Manipulation policies can choose better actions if they anticipate how objects move and how spatial relations change, but world action models usually predict future images and leave the 3D geometry implicit. The new method uses a frozen 3D foundation model at training time to extract feature differences from aligned current and future observations, then distills those targets into spatial transition queries. At deployment it predicts the queries without future observations or the 3D teacher. It is more robust on LIBERO and RoboTwin2.0, reaching 95.01% average success on the latter's full setting. On six real-world manipulation tasks, the mean task score is 85.8%.

7. DiWALI: Diffusion Policy Learning in World Models via Latent Action Inference from Human and Robot Play

UUniversity of FreiburgTechnical University of MunichToyota Motor Europe

Action-labeled robot data is scarce, while human play and video are abundant but come without robot commands. The new method infers latent actions from consecutive frames with an inverse dynamics model, trains a world model without action labels, and then trains a decoder on robot play to map those latent actions to real commands. Skill policies are first trained from action-free human demonstrations and then improved with reinforcement learning in imagination. On the CALVIN benchmark, policies trained only from human demonstrations substantially outperform baselines trained with robot demonstrations. Adding human play to world-model training also yields stronger policies after fine-tuning than the same amount of robot play alone.

8. ScaleEvals: Scaling Generalist Policy Evaluation through Reset-Free Autonomous Play

Evaluating generalist robot policies usually depends on manual resets and human grading, and small fixed task suites give little diagnostic coverage. The new protocol lets a robot fleet play continuously without resets, uses a vision-language model to generate scene-grounded tasks with difficulty and skill tags, and uses another model to grade progress, agreeing with human graders 90.7% of the time. Across more than three thousand physical rollouts on DROID and YAM, each station completes 23.0 net rollouts per hour and active human labor falls 98%. The ranking of five generalist policies matches held-out manual evaluation closely, and recombined evidence can also predict where the order changes on unseen prompts.

9. H-VLA: Hierarchical Vision-Language-Action Model with Key-Action Reasoning and Motion Planning in a Unified Action Space

SSamsung

Many vision-language-action models still map language and images directly to dense actions, which can wash out the semantic reasoning inherited from pretraining and makes them fragile when object pose, scene, embodiment, or camera viewpoint changes. The new method splits high-level key-action reasoning from low-level motion: it first predicts the next subgoal, then generates dense actions conditioned on that key action, in a unified camera-centric action space shared across datasets, embodiments, and viewpoints. On SimplerEnv, it reaches 91% on Google Robot visual matching and 84% on variant aggregation. On Agilex real-robot tasks, it improves over the strongest baseline by 47 points under out-of-distribution positions, with further gains in-distribution and under out-of-distribution scenes or objects.

10. A³-VLA: Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation

NeurIPS 2026USTCSShanghai AI LaboratoryNNUAAHKUSTHKUST (Guangzhou)

When vision-language-action models learn to predict actions, they often lack fine-grained perceptual supervision, so they lean on task-irrelevant visual shortcuts and look away from the target object and the key interaction region. The new method derives visual guidance from the manipulation process itself: it first forms candidate regions for the target and the robot from interaction-induced visual changes, refines them with a segmentation model offline, and then trains with two objectives. One encourages enough attention on task-critical regions when language or robot-state tokens query the image, and the other aligns candidate target representations with the instruction while separating them from irrelevant regions. On simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, it consistently improves several VLA backbones, with an average success-rate gain of 6.5% to 25.2% across benchmarks.
