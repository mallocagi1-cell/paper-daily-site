Today's retrieval track asks how finely evidence should enter the index, how knowledge should move when the encoder or the institution changes, and whether an agent's searchable world should grow with its ability. Visually rich pages and chart-heavy PDFs are a poor fit for text-first pipelines; region-level or multi-vector matching can align a query with the useful part of a page. Cold-start encoder changes and federated retrieval try to avoid full re-embedding and raw-document transfer. When training search agents, tasks and evidence need to grow together along the capability frontier. Video search still fails by picking the wrong video, and long instructional videos still lose inspectable evidence, so finding the right object and keeping a checkable moment both matter. Search skill can also be taught during pretraining rather than only in post-training.

1. When Visually Rich Documents Meet Multi-Vector Retrieval for Region-level Retrieval-Augmented Generation

HHong Kong Polytechnic University

Vision-based retrieval-augmented generation over visually rich documents often encodes a whole page as one coarse vector and then feeds the full page image to the generator, which can bury local evidence and waste visual tokens. A region-level approach first splits each page into semantically complete regions, retrieves with late-interaction multi-vector matching, and uses those matching scores to estimate each region's contribution so only the relevant regions are rearranged into a compact visual context. Across five benchmarks, it raises average nDCG@5 by 3.47 points over VDocRAG, the strongest single-vector visual baseline, when both use Phi3V. Region-level augmentation also cuts visual tokens by 43% to 62% relative to feeding the full page. Semantically complete regions can serve as a shared basis for matching and evidence selection, keeping retrieval and generation quality with less visual input. Table-style numeric questions can still suffer when headers are cropped away from their values.

2. ERA: Embed, Route, Adapt for Cold-Start Cross-Model Latent Retrieval

Google

A production retrieval index is tied to the embedding model that built it, so switching the query encoder usually means re-embedding the whole corpus. At cold start there are no query logs, and every document encoded by the new model still incurs compute or API cost. Google spends a small anchor budget on the most informative documents, chosen from the existing target index by cosine farthest-point sampling, trains a lightweight adapter, and uses a deterministic router to escalate the rare hard queries to the target encoder. Across five BEIR datasets, the system matches or outperforms pointwise L2 and embedding-conversion baselines, with relative nDCG@10 gains of up to 35% on Arguana. At a 20% nominal escalation budget, end-to-end query latency falls from roughly 160 ms to 38 to 86 ms, and escalations concentrate on the queries where the projection fails most.

3. The Dual-Tier Sentiment Framework: Scalable Multimodal UX Attribution in Web Search

Google

Keeping user experience in large-scale web search usually depends on surveys that are too slow for rapid A/B tests, while automated metrics often miss visual and spatial problems on the results page. Google uses a two-tier framework for scalable attribution. The first tier fine-tunes a Gemini XS model on telemetry features to detect whole-page satisfaction regressions. The second tier uses a multimodal model to inspect the rendered layout of result blocks and estimate how much effort a user needs to reach a satisfactory result. On more than half a million held-out records, the first tier raises PR-AUC by 9.8%. In production, measurement latency falls from weeks to hours, and survey overhead drops by more than 40%. Screening for page-level regressions first, then diagnosing local friction, keeps evaluation fast enough for experiments while still explaining where the problem is.

4. Search-RSI: Expanding Search Worlds Along Multimodal Agents&#39; Capability Frontier

HHunan UniversityNYU

Multimodal search agents are often trained on growing multi-hop tasks while the searchable world is held fixed. A new question can be well formed and verifiable, yet its evidence may be missing from the retrieval backend, so the agent issues the right query, retrieves nothing, and is penalized as if the policy were at fault. A recursive improvement loop grows tasks together with their evidence along the agent's capability frontier: the current policy diagnoses regions it has only partly mastered, then decides whether each new task should feed reinforcement learning or become a hint-guided demonstration of its own. On nine multimodal search benchmarks, it raises the average accuracy of the recipe it builds on by 9.8 and 9.5 points at the 9B and 27B scales, and 7.0 of those points are out of reach for a world that is merely made consistent. The searchable world has to grow with the policy's ability, rather than accumulating new questions against a frozen index.

5. DiTraR: Diverse Scorers and Transcript-Guided Re-ranking for Video Corpus Moment Retrieval

University of Central FloridaKKaunas University of Technology

When a system must find, in a large collection of untrimmed videos, the video and time span described by a natural-language query, most top-ranked errors of a strong corpus-level scorer come from choosing the wrong video rather than from imprecise boundaries. Re-weighting the evidence that scorer already uses does not fix those errors. One approach attacks video selection from two sides. Independently trained witness-interval scorers average their energies and search every candidate interval of every corpus video exactly, and a frozen language model then reads the time-stamped dialogue of the highest-ranked videos to reorder moments without changing their boundaries. With all settings frozen on development data, R@1 at intersection-over-union 0.5 reaches 24.36 on the TVR validation split, 4.13 points above a single scorer. The ensemble mainly helps visual queries, the transcript verifier mainly helps dialogue queries, and the two gains largely add up.

6. Search From the Start: Search-Native Pretraining for Language Models

EPFLUniversity of Toronto

Language models are increasingly given search tools at inference time, yet pretraining is still mostly about predicting static text, and search skill is usually added only in post-training. Search-native pretraining inserts web-search queries into ordinary text corpora at scale and retrieves responses from a local index, so the model learns to issue search calls and to continue generation from the retrieved evidence while it is still doing language modeling. Across Wikipedia and DCLM-Edu, and from hundreds of millions to a few billion parameters, the advantage remains after supervised fine-tuning of both the search-pretrained models and conventionally pretrained counterparts. On the average of eight knowledge-intensive QA tasks, a 1.4B search-pretrained model reaches 30.3% in the base setting, ahead of a FLOP-matched 3.3B standard model at 23.9%. Post-training recipes that introduce search later do not fully recover what was learned during pretraining.

7. EP-LVMQA: Evidence-Preserving Coarse-to-Fine Localization for Low-Quality Long Video Moment Question Answering

NTUByteDanceBeihang UniversityWestlake University

Long instructional videos are widely used in medical education and procedural training, but their evidence is often incomplete: subtitles may be missing, speech recognition noisy, on-screen text sparse, and a decisive action may appear only briefly without narration. Typical video question answering systems return labels or free-form text, so the supporting moment is hard to inspect. An evidence-preserving coarse-to-fine method first builds an uncertainty-aware multimodal evidence timeline, then plans coarsely and localizes finely, passing scores, cues, and source-confidence signals between the two stages on an evidence card. On MedVidQA, recall of the top moment at intersection-over-union 0.5 reaches 26.1, 6.3 points above the strongest external result. Under matched low-quality perturbations, average relative degradation falls from 14.6% to 11.1%. Absolute audit rates remain too low for dependable human-facing deployment.

8. Beyond Raw Context Transfer: Representation-based Federated Retrieval-Augmented Generation

NeurIPS 2026University of OxfordNational University of Singapore

Retrieval-augmented generation usually assumes a centralized corpus, but in sensitive domains such as healthcare, raw documents often cannot be shared across institutions. Decentralized systems that pass the retrieved passages themselves expose readable content and leave a long context at inference time. A representation-based federated approach keeps raw documents on the clients that own them, exchanges only compact latent representations during cross-client retrieval, and uses a collaboratively trained projector to turn those retrieval embeddings into representation tokens a frozen generator can read. Across decentralized visual and textual question-answering benchmarks, it consistently outperforms direct inference and local-only retrieval. On SLAKE-EN under the more heterogeneous split, accuracy is 14.13 points above direct inference, and retrieval-context length is about 19 to 398 times shorter than raw-context transfer. Residual leakage from exchanged representations remains, so this is not end-to-end confidentiality.

9. How to extract meaning from charts and tables in PDFs

Weaviate

When pitch decks, annual reports, or papers are fed through a typical retrieval-augmented generation pipeline, the system first extracts text, then chunks and embeds it. A question about a revenue trend may come back with unrelated bullet points, because the answer sits in a bar chart that the index never saw. Late-interaction multi-vector models render each page as an image and encode it as a set of vectors, one per image patch, so a query can match the regions that matter and skip text extraction and chunking entirely. On Weaviate Cloud, a hosted module ingested four quarterly investor presentations totaling 92 pages in about a minute and a half, and example questions returned the exact chart page that answered them. Charts and tables are primary information, not noise to be fixed by better optical character recognition. For text-dominated corpora, a text pipeline may still be cheaper and good enough.

10. Weaviate 1.39 Release

Weaviate

The first page of a hybrid search often repeats nearly identical passages, and a hard filter that prefers in-stock or recently updated items can drop a highly relevant object that fails the condition. This Weaviate release moves query-time boost rescoring and maximal marginal relevance diversity selection to general availability. Boost re-ranks candidates against conditions without removing anything, and diversity selection trades relevance against variety on hybrid search as well as vector search. When in-stock and recent items are preferred, the original top match can still stay first. A preview of four-bit rotational quantization stores a 1536-dimension vector in 784 bytes, 7.84 times smaller than raw float32. HNSW snapshots also become automatic, cutting commit-log disk usage and speeding up startup.
